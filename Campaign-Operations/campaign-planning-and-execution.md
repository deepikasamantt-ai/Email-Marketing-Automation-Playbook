# Campaign Planning and Execution Playbook

## Overview

Effective email campaign management requires structured planning, clear ownership, coordinated execution, quality controls, and measurable outcomes.

This playbook provides a repeatable framework for managing campaigns from the initial brief through deployment and post-campaign analysis. It is designed for B2B and B2C marketing teams using email marketing and marketing automation platforms.

## 1. Campaign Intake and Brief

Every campaign should begin with a documented brief that defines its purpose, audience, requirements, and success criteria.

### Essential campaign brief elements

- **Campaign objective:** What business or marketing outcome should the campaign achieve?
- **Target audience:** Which contacts or customer segments should receive the communication?
- **Campaign type:** Newsletter, promotional email, lead nurture, onboarding, product announcement, event invitation, or another format.
- **Core message:** What should recipients understand or do after reading the email?
- **Call to action (CTA):** What is the primary action the recipient should take?
- **Content and creative requirements:** Copy, design, assets, landing pages, and personalization needs.
- **Channels and dependencies:** Email platform, CRM integration, website, analytics, and supporting systems.
- **Timeline:** Brief approval, content delivery, QA, stakeholder sign-off, deployment, and reporting dates.
- **Success metrics:** Delivery, engagement, conversion, registrations, qualified leads, or other relevant outcomes.
- **Stakeholders and approvals:** Requester, campaign owner, content and design teams, technical teams, and approvers.

**Planning principle:** Clarify requirements and ownership before execution begins. Unresolved dependencies should be documented rather than silently carried into the production timeline.

## 2. Audience and Campaign Strategy

Translate the campaign objective into a clear audience and communication plan.

### Audience planning

- Define inclusion and exclusion criteria.
- Confirm consent, subscription status, and applicable suppression rules.
- Identify lifecycle stage, engagement level, geography, language, and relevant customer attributes.
- Review overlapping segments and potential duplicate communications.
- Estimate audience size and investigate unexpected changes.
- Confirm that the selected audience aligns with the campaign objective.

### Messaging strategy

- Align the subject line and preheader with the campaign message.
- Keep the primary CTA consistent with the intended recipient action.
- Use relevant personalization and dynamic content where appropriate.
- Match content to the audience's lifecycle stage and level of familiarity with the brand.
- Ensure that landing page messaging is consistent with the email.

Audience selection should be driven by campaign relevance and eligibility, not simply by the number of contacts available to send.

## 3. Campaign Timeline and Workback Plan

Build the execution timeline backward from the intended deployment date.

| Phase | Key activities | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | Requirements, objectives, stakeholders | Approved campaign brief |
| Planning | Audience, messaging, timeline, dependencies | Execution plan |
| Production | Copy, design, assets, landing pages | Campaign assets |
| Configuration | Audience setup, tracking, personalization, scheduling | Configured campaign |
| QA | Content, links, rendering, audience, settings | QA results and issue log |
| Approval | Stakeholder review and release authorization | Documented sign-off |
| Deployment | Schedule or release campaign | Deployment confirmation |
| Monitoring | Delivery checks and initial performance review | Monitoring record |
| Reporting | Results analysis and recommendations | Campaign report |

Timelines should reflect campaign complexity, audience size, language requirements, approval dependencies, and the consequences of a deployment error.

For urgent requests, identify which activities can run in parallel without compromising essential QA or approval controls.

## 4. Stakeholder Coordination and Ownership

Campaign execution often involves multiple teams working toward a shared deadline. Clear ownership reduces delays, duplicated work, and conflicting instructions.

Typical responsibilities include:

- **Campaign or project manager:** Timeline, dependencies, status updates, risk tracking, and cross-functional coordination.
- **Marketing requester:** Business objective, audience requirements, messaging direction, and final business approval.
- **Content and creative teams:** Copy, design, assets, and revisions.
- **Marketing operations or automation team:** Platform configuration, audience selection, tracking, testing, scheduling, and deployment.
- **Technical or web team:** Landing pages, forms, integrations, and technical troubleshooting.
- **Legal or compliance stakeholders:** Review of applicable privacy, consent, regulatory, and brand requirements.
- **Reporting owner:** Performance analysis and post-campaign recommendations.

Responsibilities vary by organization. Assign one accountable owner for each critical deliverable and approval.

### Communication practices

- Maintain a central campaign tracker.
- Record task owners, deadlines, dependencies, and status.
- Document scope changes and their impact on delivery.
- Escalate blockers early, with a clear description of the decision required.
- Confirm important approvals in a traceable format.
- Communicate deployment readiness and any residual risks before release.

## 5. Campaign Build and Configuration

Once the brief, audience, and assets are ready, configure the campaign in the selected platform.

### Configuration checklist

- Select the correct campaign and email asset.
- Confirm sender name, sender address, and reply-to details.
- Apply the approved audience and exclusion logic.
- Configure personalization and dynamic content.
- Add tracking parameters according to the agreed naming convention.
- Validate CTA destinations and landing pages.
- Confirm required subscription, consent, and unsubscribe controls.
- Set the intended deployment date, time, and time zone.
- Review frequency limits and potential overlap with other campaigns.
- Confirm that integrations and required data fields are available.

Use a documented configuration standard wherever possible. Reusable templates and naming conventions improve consistency, traceability, and handoffs between team members.

## 6. Quality Assurance and Release Readiness

QA should be treated as a formal campaign phase rather than a last-minute visual check.

Review:

- Copy, subject line, preheader, sender details, and footer.
- Desktop and mobile rendering.
- Personalization tokens and conditional content.
- Primary and secondary links.
- Landing pages, forms, and tracking parameters.
- Audience counts, inclusion criteria, and suppression rules.
- Platform settings and scheduling.
- Required stakeholder approvals.

Record defects, assign owners, retest corrections, and confirm that the final production version matches the approved version.

Campaigns with unresolved critical issues should not proceed without an authorized, documented decision.

## 7. Managing Changes and Delivery Risks

Late changes can affect content accuracy, QA coverage, approvals, and deployment timelines. Use a consistent change-control process.

When a change is requested:

1. Document the requested change and its business rationale.
2. Assess the impact on content, audience, configuration, tracking, and timeline.
3. Identify the tasks and approvals that must be repeated.
4. Communicate the impact to the relevant stakeholders.
5. Obtain the required approval.
6. Implement and retest the change.
7. Update the campaign record and confirm release readiness.

### Common risks and mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Late content or creative delivery | Establish deadlines and escalation points |
| Incorrect audience selection | Validate criteria, exclusions, and audience counts |
| Broken or expired destinations | Test links and verify destination availability |
| Missing approvals | Define approvers and approval deadlines at intake |
| Last-minute configuration changes | Repeat relevant QA and confirm the final version |
| Conflicting deployment schedules | Review dependencies and coordinate release windows |
| Tracking inconsistencies | Standardize campaign naming and tracking conventions |
| Unexpected platform or integration issues | Test dependencies and document contingency procedures |

When a deadline conflicts with essential QA or compliance requirements, escalate the trade-off rather than treating the deadline as automatic authorization to deploy.

## 8. Deployment and Monitoring

Before deployment, confirm that the campaign is approved, the intended audience and asset are selected, and the final configuration has passed the required checks.

After release:

- Confirm deployment status.
- Monitor initial delivery and bounce indicators.
- Review complaints, unsubscribes, and other relevant warning signals.
- Investigate unexpected results or technical errors.
- Notify stakeholders of material issues.
- Follow the organization's incident-response process if intervention is required.

The level of monitoring should reflect campaign risk, audience size, business importance, and platform capabilities.

## 9. Post-Campaign Reporting

Evaluate performance against the original campaign objective.

### Recommended reporting dimensions

- Delivery rate and bounce rate.
- Unique click-through rate and click-to-open rate, where available.
- Conversion rate and completed target actions.
- Registrations, qualified leads, or pipeline contribution where measurable.
- Unsubscribe and complaint rates.
- Performance by relevant audience segment or content variant.
- Comparison with an appropriate historical baseline or campaign target.

Open rates should be interpreted cautiously because privacy features and automated activity can distort the metric.

### Reporting workflow

1. Confirm that sufficient time has passed for results to mature.
2. Validate the reporting data and tracking setup.
3. Compare results with the campaign objective and agreed targets.
4. Identify meaningful patterns and unexpected outcomes.
5. Document findings, limitations, and likely contributing factors.
6. Recommend specific improvements for future campaigns.

Distinguish observed results from assumptions about causation. A performance difference alone does not establish why a campaign succeeded or underperformed.

## 10. Reusable Campaign Readiness Checklist

- [ ] Campaign objective and brief approved
- [ ] Audience and exclusion criteria confirmed
- [ ] Content and creative assets approved
- [ ] Timeline, owners, and dependencies documented
- [ ] Landing pages and supporting assets ready
- [ ] Platform configuration completed
- [ ] Tracking and reporting requirements validated
- [ ] QA completed and defects resolved
- [ ] Required approvals recorded
- [ ] Final production version confirmed
- [ ] Deployment completed and monitored
- [ ] Results reviewed and learnings documented

## Guiding Principles

- Plan around the campaign objective, not just the send date.
- Establish ownership and dependencies early.
- Make changes traceable and assess their operational impact.
- Protect audience accuracy, consent, and customer experience.
- Use risk-based QA and documented release approvals.
- Measure performance against defined objectives.
- Turn campaign learnings into improvements in future execution.
